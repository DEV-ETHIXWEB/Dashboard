# The EthixWeb Dashboard

One place where your project lives — what we're building, where it stands
today, what you've asked us for, and what you've paid. No more asking for a
status update and waiting a day for the answer.

This document has two halves. The first is what **you** see when you sign in.
The second is what **we** run behind it, so you know what the team can handle
without leaving this one system.

---

## Part one — what you'll be using

### Your four tabs

The dashboard opens on four things, because in practice there are only four
questions a client ever needs answered.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Home** | Where everything stands, at a glance | Your projects, anything waiting on you, and what changed since you last looked. |
| **Work** | What we're building, as we build it | Live progress on each piece of work, with the stage it's in and who's on it. Updates appear on your screen within a second of us moving something — you don't refresh. |
| **Requests** | Ask us for something, then watch it move | Raise a request, get a written confirmation immediately, then see every reply, status change, and resolution against it. |
| **Money** | Invoices, receipts, what's settled | Every invoice and payment, with the official receipt beside it. |

Behind **More**, one tap away: **Chat** (talk to your project team in
writing, on the record), **Projects**, **Websites** (your domains — registrar,
hosting, SSL, renewal dates), **Documents** (reports and files we've prepared
for you, to download any time), **Spending** (what the budget has gone on, by
category), and **Alerts**.

### It works properly on your phone

Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app — full screen, its own
icon, straight into your dashboard. It's the same dashboard as on a laptop,
laid out for a thumb, not a shrunken desktop page.

If you sign out, nothing of yours is left cached on the device.

### You'll hear from us by email, automatically

You don't have to log in to stay informed. Real emails — properly designed,
not system noise — go out at the moments that actually matter:

- **You raised a request** → written confirmation, with your reference.
- **Someone replied, or the status moved** → the update, in full.
- **Your request is resolved** → what was done.
- **Where your work stands** → a periodic summary of the whole picture.
- **A payment went through** → your receipt.
- **A payment failed** → what failed, and what to do, before it becomes a problem.
- **A domain is coming up for renewal** → in good time, not the week it lapses.

Everything sent to you is sent from our own verified domain, so it lands in
your inbox rather than a spam folder.

### Signing in

Two steps, every time, for everyone — including our own staff:

1. Your password.
2. A 6-digit code, emailed to you the moment the password is accepted.

If you'd rather not deal with a password at all, we can send you a **one-tap
sign-in link**. Open it and you're in. It works once, expires in 15 minutes,
and only we can issue it — there's no "email me a link" button for anyone who
happens to know your address.

We never see or choose your password. The system generates it, shows it to the
person setting up your account exactly once, and stores only a scrambled
version that can't be read back — not by us, not by anyone with access to the
database.

## Part two — what we handle for you

Everything below is run by our team from the same system, so nothing about
your account lives in someone's inbox or a spreadsheet.

### The work itself

- **Projects and tasks** — the full plan, who owns each piece, and what stage
  it's in. What you see under **Work** is a view onto the real thing the team
  is working from, not a summary someone retypes for you.
- **Requests and response times** — every request you raise is tracked against
  a first-response deadline. The system chases *us* about it: if a reply is
  falling due, or has been missed, the team is alerted automatically rather
  than discovering it at the next meeting.
- **Client messages** — written conversation kept against your account, so a
  decision made in passing is still findable in six months.

### Your websites and domains

One record per site: platform, hosting provider and region, registrar, SSL
status, DNS status, and the expiry date — with renewal reminders that go out
to you automatically as the date approaches. A lapsed domain is one of the few
genuinely expensive mistakes in this business, and it isn't left to memory.

### Money

Stripe is the ledger, not us. Invoices, charges, and subscriptions are mirrored
into the dashboard, and every figure you see is drawn from that mirror with a
link to Stripe's own receipt beside it — so your screen and the payment
processor can't quietly disagree.

**Your card details are entered on Stripe's own hosted pages and never touch
our servers.** We couldn't store them if we wanted to.

Alongside it: **budget tracking** by category, so "what has the retainer
actually gone on" has an answer on screen.

### Documents

Reports and files we prepare are uploaded once and available to you to download
whenever you want them — stored in Google Drive where that's set up.

### Access control — who sees what

- We issue your login from an admin console. Each one can carry an **expiry
  date**, after which it stops working until we deliberately renew it.
- We can switch individual sections **on or off per client**. If a section is
  off for you, its tab disappears entirely and the next one moves up — no dead
  links, no "you don't have permission" screens.
- Our own staff are scoped by role too. Sales, project managers, and employees
  each see the part of the workspace their job needs, and no more.

### Controls on ourselves

This is the part most portals don't have, and it's worth knowing about:

- **A second signature.** Sensitive changes can't be made by one person acting
  alone. An administrator can *propose* the change; every approver is notified;
  nothing touches the data until someone else signs it off. Nobody can approve
  their own request.
- **A full audit log.** Every change written by anyone is recorded — who,
  what, when — and readable by the account that owns the workspace.
- **A mail record.** Every message the system has ever sent you is logged with
  its delivery status, so "did that ever actually reach you?" is a question
  with a real answer. Sign-in codes and credentials are deliberately *excluded*
  from that record — they're delivered to you and never stored anywhere
  readable afterwards.
- **Staff sign-in is held to the same standard as yours.** Administrators go
  through the same password-plus-code step, and an administrator's sign-in code
  cannot be revealed to any other administrator.

### Integrations

The dashboard connects to the tools the team already works in — **ClickUp** for
task management and **Slack** for day-to-day coordination, including a
dedicated channel per client where that's useful. Work stays in sync instead of
being copied between systems by hand.

## In short

You get one honest view of your project that updates itself, tells you when
something changes without being asked, and doesn't depend on anyone remembering
to send an update.

We get a system that enforces the things we'd otherwise promise: response times
that are tracked, renewals that can't be forgotten, permissions that are
explicit, and a record of every change and every message — including the ones
we send you.
